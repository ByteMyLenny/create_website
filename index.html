About me

I am Kylin Crofford, a senior undergraduate in the Computer Engineering department at Texas Tech University, expecting to graduate in December 2027. I originally started in Computer Science but switched to Computer Engineering because I wanted to go deeper into hardware alongside software as I've always been drawn to understanding systems at every level, not just the code running on top of them.

My academic and project work spans a wide range of languages and tools, with C# and Java as my favorites. Projects like my Raycast-Guided Targeting System, Modular Arm System, and Autonomous Traffic Light System reflect what drives me most: solving problems and optimizing systems, whether that's a physical mechanism or a piece of software architecture. I also built a Java and SQL-based Engineering Inventory Management System that's core to the type of practical, efficiency-focused work I enjoy.

This past summer, I completed an internship at BAE Systems in Fort Worth as a Software/Hardware Engineer Intern, where I worked on avionics and defense engineering software and hardware. I engineered a Java desktop application backed by a SQL database that automated engineering inventory management, cutting a three-week manual workflow down to under five minutes, and supported hardware identification and inventory comparison for engineering teams. BAE Systems has expressed interest in bringing me on full-time after graduation.

Beyond the classroom and lab, I've volunteered at robotics events like GEAR Robotics, supporting competitions and mentoring student participants. When I'm not programming or working on circuitry, I spend my time practicing saxophone, playing video games, and training at Texas Tech's Matador MMA Club.
